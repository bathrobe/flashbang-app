# Example questions

Joe adds to this file. Each example has one line on why it is good or bad. All are from Lecture 1. A ✓ marks a correct option.

## Good

**Multiple choice**

> Athens had ten generals at a time. How did they stand in relation to one another?
>
> - One was chosen as commander for each campaign
> - They were equal in rank and rivals in the field ✓
> - They took the supreme command in rotation
> - The oldest held authority over the others

Why: the options are the same length and the same register, and each wrong one is what someone might half remember.

**Multi-select**

> Which of these sources for Athenian democracy does Garland say should be read with caution?
>
> - Plutarch's biographies ✓
> - The Constitution of the Athenians ✓
> - The tribute lists
> - Thucydides' speeches ✓
> - The owl coins

Why: it needs the lecture, not common sense, and the learner has to weigh each option on its own.

**True/false**

> Garland says the Old Oligarch praises Athenian democracy because its author admired the demos.

False. Why: it states a believable misreading, so the learner has to recall the point (the praise is ironic).

**Explanation**

> There was no accountability in the Assembly: once it had voted for a proposal, the Assembly took the responsibility, and the proposer faced no charge. Garland sets this beside the absence of professional politicians: speakers were just men who got up to speak.

Why: it states the atom's idea in full, then adds one piece of context from the lecture.

## Bad

**The correct answer is the longest** (Joe's hard rule 1)

> Why was the generalship elected?
>
> - Tradition
> - It paid well
> - Because military command was the one office where expertise could not be left to chance ✓
> - Religion

Why: you can pick the answer by its length without knowing anything.

**Absolute words in the wrong answers** (Joe's hard rule 2)

> How did Athenian democracy come about?
>
> - It was entirely the work of the poor
> - It never involved aristocrats
> - It evolved gradually, largely without violence ✓
> - Every reform was won by force

Why: the wrong options are absolute and the right one is hedged, so the hedge gives it away.

**Answerable from common sense**

> True or false: Athens was named for the goddess Athena.

Why: nobody needs the lecture for this, and it leads back to no atom.

**More than one defensible answer**

> Which was a feature of Athenian democracy?
>
> - Selection by lot ✓
> - Compulsory military service
> - Taxes on brothels
> - Trial by jury

Why: the lecture supports all four.

**The explanation restates the answer**

> Correct: the generals were elected.

Why: it teaches nothing. It should give the whole idea (the lot for every office, election for the one that needed expertise) and one more thing from the lecture.
